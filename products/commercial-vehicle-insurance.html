Commercial Vehicle Insurance in India

If your vehicle earns money — a cab, a delivery tempo, a goods truck, a tourist bus — it needs a commercial policy, not a private one. Here's what's actually different, and how to get it right.

IRDAI Licensed All Vehicle Classes Permit Guidance

Why Commercial Vehicles Need a Different Policy

A private car policy is priced and underwritten assuming personal, non-earning use. The moment a vehicle is used for hire — carrying paying passengers, goods, or running a delivery route — its risk profile changes: more distance covered, more hours on the road, and exposure to third parties who are paying customers rather than family. Using a private policy for commercial activity is one of the most common reasons claims get rejected outright, regardless of how the accident happened.

Permits & Registration — What You Need Alongside Insurance

Commercial vehicles must carry a valid permit matching their use — a National Permit or State Permit for goods carriers, and a Taxi Permit or All India Tourist Permit for passenger vehicles — issued by the Regional Transport Office (RTO). Most insurers will ask to see your permit and commercial registration (typically a yellow number plate, or white for some tourist-permit vehicles) before issuing or renewing a commercial policy. Keep both documents current — a lapsed permit can complicate a claim even if the insurance itself is valid.

What's Covered

  • Third-party liability: Legally mandatory, covering injury or property damage to third parties, including passengers in a passenger-carrying vehicle.
  • Own damage: Covers your vehicle against accident, fire, theft and natural disaster — optional but recommended for any commercial vehicle with meaningful resale value.
  • Goods-in-transit (optional add-on): Protects the value of cargo being transported, separate from the vehicle itself — essential for owners transporting high-value goods.
  • Personal accident cover: Mandatory ₹15 lakh cover for the owner-driver, same as private vehicles.
  • Passenger cover (optional): For cabs and buses, additional passenger personal accident cover can be added beyond the driver's mandatory cover.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between commercial vehicle insurance and private car insurance?

Commercial vehicle insurance covers vehicles used for hire, reward, or business purposes — cabs, trucks, buses, tempos and goods carriers — and legally requires a valid commercial permit alongside the policy. Premiums are typically higher than private car insurance because commercial vehicles cover more distance, carry passengers or goods for payment, and face higher usage-related risk. Using a private car for cab or delivery services without commercial insurance can void your claim entirely.

Do I need separate insurance for a cab or taxi used with aggregators like Ola or Uber?

Yes — a private car policy will not cover an accident while the vehicle is being used commercially for ride-hailing. You need a commercial passenger-carrying vehicle policy, and most insurers also require the vehicle to be registered with a commercial number plate and hold a valid taxi/tourist permit.

What does goods carrier insurance cover for trucks and tempos?

Goods carrier insurance covers third-party liability, own-damage to the vehicle, and typically an option for goods-in-transit cover (protecting the cargo itself, sold separately or as an add-on). It's priced by gross vehicle weight (GVW) and usage type — mandatory third-party rates for goods carriers are fixed by IRDAI and scale with tonnage.
