Car Insurance Claim Settlement Ratio 2026: Compare All Insurers

September 2026 · 8 min read · By Vikash Aggarwal

Most people shop for car insurance on premium alone. That's backwards. A policy is only worth what it pays out when you actually need it — and claim settlement data is the closest thing to a report card on how an insurer behaves when your car is damaged, stolen, or written off. After 22 years placing motor policies for Indian families, I can tell you the ₹500–1,000/year you save by picking the cheapest quote is meaningless if the insurer fights you on a ₹2 lakh claim.

This guide compares claim settlement data across India's major car insurers, explains what the numbers actually mean, and tells you where to verify them yourself.

Claim Settlement Ratio vs Incurred Claim Ratio: Know the Difference

These two terms get used interchangeably, but they measure different things — and mixing them up leads to bad comparisons:

  • Claim Settlement Ratio (CSR): The percentage of claims an insurer settles out of all claims it receives in a period. This is the standard metric for life and health insurance, where IRDAI publishes it annually per insurer.
  • Incurred Claim Ratio (ICR): Total claims paid divided by total premiums earned for a line of business, expressed as a percentage. This is the metric most commonly reported for motor and general insurance. An ICR above 100% means the insurer paid out more in claims than it collected in premiums for that segment — generally a sign of strong claims payout, though it also reflects pricing and portfolio mix.

When you see a car insurer's "claim settlement ratio" quoted at 99% or 102%, check which metric is actually being reported — the two aren't directly comparable across insurers unless you know which one you're looking at.

How to Verify Claim Settlement Data Yourself

Don't take any single source's word for it — including ours. Here's where the data actually comes from:

  • IRDAI Annual Report / Handbook on Indian Insurance Statistics: The primary regulatory source, published once a year with insurer-wise claim and incurred claim ratios.
  • Insurer disclosures: Most general insurers publish claims data on their own websites or in investor disclosures.
  • Aggregators: Platforms like PolicyBazaar and comparison sites (including this one) compile insurer data into side-by-side tables — useful for a quick view, but always check the reporting period, since ratios move year to year.

💡 A high claim ratio tells you an insurer pays out — it doesn't guarantee your specific claim will be smooth. Garage network density near you, the add-ons you've bought (zero depreciation, engine protect), and how promptly you report an incident all matter just as much.

Beyond the Ratio: What Else Should Influence Your Choice

1. Garage Network Near You

A 99% claim ratio means little if the nearest cashless garage is 40 km away. SBI General (20,000+), Reliance General (8,500+) and Tata AIG (7,500+) lead on network size.

2. Claims Turnaround Time

Digit's digital-first process settles some claims in as little as 7 minutes for straightforward cases — turnaround time matters as much as the eventual payout for day-to-day convenience.

3. Add-On Coverage

Zero depreciation, engine protect and return-to-invoice cover often determine your actual payout amount far more than a percentage-point difference in claim ratio between two well-rated insurers.

⚠️ Watch for stale data: claim and incurred claim ratios are reported annually and can shift meaningfully year to year, especially for smaller insurers. Always check the reporting period before comparing two insurers' numbers.
