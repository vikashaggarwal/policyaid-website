Model-Specific Guidance

Car Insurance by Model — What Changes for Your Car

Your car's IDV, theft risk, and the add-ons worth paying for all shift by segment. Here's what to know before insuring popular models sold in India today.

Why the Car Model Matters for Insurance

Two cars insured for the same ₹5 lakh sum insured can have very different real-world protection needs. A Maruti Suzuki Alto and a Mahindra XUV700 both qualify for "comprehensive cover," but the Alto's IDV might be ₹3-4 lakh while the XUV700's runs ₹18-25 lakh — and the add-ons that matter (theft protection vs. sensor/ADAS repair cost, for instance) are completely different. Below is a segment-by-segment breakdown for popular models on Indian roads in 2026.

Hatchbacks — Maruti Suzuki, Entry-Level Models

Maruti Suzuki Baleno, WagonR, Alto, and similar hatchbacks are India's most common vehicles, which cuts both ways: repair parts and network garage coverage are excellent everywhere, but these models are also historically more targeted for theft in dense urban areas simply due to volume and resale demand for parts. Premiums are the lowest in this segment — typically ₹4,000-7,000/year comprehensive for a mid-spec variant.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the car model actually affect my insurance premium?

Yes, significantly. Your car's IDV (Insured Declared Value) is based on its ex-showroom price and depreciation schedule, so a Mahindra Thar or Toyota Fortuner will have a far higher IDV — and premium — than a Maruti Alto or WagonR. Repair cost, spare parts availability, and theft-risk profile for that specific model also factor into pricing beyond just the IDV.

Which car models are most frequently stolen in India, and does that affect insurance?

Popular, easily resold models — historically Maruti Suzuki hatchbacks and certain Mahindra and Hyundai SUVs — have tended to see higher theft rates in some cities simply due to how common they are and how easily parts move in the grey market. This doesn't usually change your listed premium directly, but it's a strong reason to prioritise anti-theft devices and adequate IDV on these models.

Should I buy zero depreciation cover for a new SUV like the Kia Seltos or Hyundai Creta?

Yes — for any car under 5 years old, especially mid-size and premium SUVs like the Seltos, Creta, Grand Vitara or XUV700, zero depreciation cover is strongly recommended. These models have a high proportion of plastic, fibre and sensor-heavy body parts that depreciate fast in standard claim settlements, so zero dep materially increases your actual payout.
