Destination-Specific Guidance

Travel Insurance by Destination — What Changes by Country

Visa requirements, medical cost levels and the right sum insured all shift by where you're headed. Here's what to know before booking cover for popular destinations from India.

Why Destination Changes What You Need

A policy that's more than adequate for a week in Bali can leave you badly underinsured for a fortnight in the USA. Medical costs, visa-mandated minimums, and even the type of emergencies you're likely to need cover for (altitude sickness in Nepal vs. heatstroke in the Gulf, for instance) vary enormously by destination. Below is a region-by-region breakdown for outbound travel from India in 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need travel insurance for Dubai or other UAE trips?

It's not visa-mandated for UAE like it is for Schengen countries, but strongly recommended — private hospital costs in Dubai and Abu Dhabi are high, and UAE's compulsory health insurance system for residents doesn't cover visiting tourists. A standard international plan with USD 50,000-100,000 medical cover is usually sufficient for a short leisure or business trip.

How much travel insurance cover do I need for Southeast Asia — Thailand, Bali, Singapore, Malaysia?

USD 50,000 medical cover is typically adequate for Thailand, Bali (Indonesia), Malaysia and Vietnam, where private hospital costs are moderate compared to the West. Singapore is the exception — its healthcare costs run closer to Western levels, so USD 100,000 cover is a safer minimum there.

Is travel insurance required for Nepal, Sri Lanka, or Bhutan trips from India?

Not legally required for Indian travellers to these neighbouring countries, and often skipped since no visa is needed for short stays to Nepal. It's still worth having for trip cancellation, baggage and emergency evacuation cover — medical facilities in remote trekking areas of Nepal and Bhutan can be basic, and evacuation cover matters most here.
