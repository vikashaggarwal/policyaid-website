Model-Specific Guidance

Bike Insurance by Model — What Changes for Your Two-Wheeler

IDV, theft risk and the add-ons worth paying for all shift by segment. Here's what to know before insuring popular bike and scooter models sold in India today.

Why the Model Matters for Bike Insurance

Third-party premium is fixed by IRDAI purely on engine capacity, so that part is identical across insurers for a given cc band. But your comprehensive premium — and how much add-ons like zero depreciation actually save you — depends entirely on your specific model's IDV, parts cost and theft profile. Below is a segment-by-segment breakdown for two-wheelers commonly seen on Indian roads in 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the bike model affect my insurance premium?

Yes. Engine capacity is the biggest factor for third-party premium (fixed by IRDAI in cc bands), while IDV — and therefore own-damage premium — depends on the bike's ex-showroom price and age. A Royal Enfield Classic 350 has a far higher IDV than a Hero Splendor, so its comprehensive premium and the value of add-ons like zero depreciation are proportionally higher too.

Is Royal Enfield insurance more expensive than other bikes?

Yes, proportionally — Royal Enfield's higher ex-showroom prices mean a higher IDV and higher own-damage premium than mass-market commuters like the Splendor or Activa. Theft risk and resale demand for parts also tend to be higher for popular Royal Enfield models, making zero depreciation and anti-theft device discounts worth prioritising.

Do sports bikes like the Yamaha R15 or TVS Apache cost more to insure?

Yes — performance bikes like the R15, Apache RTR series and KTM RC/Duke range carry higher IDVs and often higher repair costs for fairings and performance parts, so zero depreciation cover is particularly valuable here given how much plastic bodywork is exposed in a fall or minor collision.
